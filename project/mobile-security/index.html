Mobile Security

Measuring how mobile and wearable platforms are actually secured in the field, starting with how long Android devices stay patched.

Project resources

  • Code & Data ↗

Security patches only protect users once they reach the device. This line of work takes a device-centric view of the Android ecosystem: instead of asking which patches Google releases, it asks which patches actually land on a given phone, how quickly, and for how long.

Android security updates

We collected and analyzed more than 360,000 OEM security updates across vendors, models, and regions, measuring support duration, update frequency, and release delay. The study shows large gaps between vendors and between regions for the same device, and quantifies how many known vulnerabilities accumulate once a device leaves support. A follow-up journal study extends the analysis to the global landscape of Android updates.

Funding and recognition

In 2021, Selcuk Uluagac’s research group received a Google ASPIRE Research Award ($112,000) for Android Security and Privacy Research. I co-led the effort with Selcuk Uluagac and Ahmet Aris. See the FIU award announcement.

The work appeared at NDSS 2024 and was presented at FTC PrivacyCon 2024, Panel 6: Mobile Device Security.

VR sensors and learned side channels

IMMERSPY (NDSS 2025) shows how zero-permission motion sensors can expose vibrations from a headset’s speakers. Signal processing produces spectrograms, and a CNN-LSTM classifies spoken digits that can reveal sensitive sequences. We also use synthetic speech generated through text-to-speech to train without labeled victim recordings, and evaluate a tone-based countermeasure.

Our XR security and privacy article places sensor leakage within the broader risks of extended-reality devices. Wearable behavioral authentication is covered in the IoT Security & Privacy project, while the AI Security project brings together the learning methods used across these systems.
